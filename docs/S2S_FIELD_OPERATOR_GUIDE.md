# Bhasha Setu — S2S Field Operator Quick Reference Guide

**Document Version:** 1.0  
**Phase:** Phase 8 — Deployment Engineering & Field-Pilot Execution Support  
**Target Audience:** Frontline Health Workers (ASHA / ANM), Primary Teachers, Field Operators  
**Principle:** Simple, non-technical instructions for high-reliability field conversations.

---

## 1. When to Speak

1. Look at your speaker card (Speaker A or Speaker B).
2. Tap the **Microphone Button** once.
3. Wait until the button pulses and the indicator says **"Listening..."** or **"Speak now"**.
4. Speak in your normal, everyday conversational voice:
   - Hold the phone approximately **10 to 20 cm (hand span)** from your mouth.
   - Do NOT shout; speaking loudly can distort the phone's microphone.
   - Speak in complete, short sentences (e.g. *"आपको बुखार कब से है?"* or *"ᱟᱢᱟᱜ ᱧᱩᱛᱩᱢ ᱪᱮᱫ?"*).

---

## 2. When to Stop Speaking

1. When you finish your thought or sentence, **simply stop talking**.
2. Keep the phone steady for a moment.
3. You do NOT need to rush. You can pause for 1 to 4 seconds while thinking; the system will stay open and wait for you.

---

## 3. What Auto-Stop Means

- Bhasha Setu has an **automatic 7-second silence auto-stop**.
- When you stop speaking, the system listens for quiet. If it detects continuous silence for 7 seconds, it automatically:
  1. Stops the microphone.
  2. Processes what you said.
  3. Translates the text into the other person's language.
  4. Speaks the translation aloud through the loudspeaker.
- **Shortcut:** If you don't want to wait 7 seconds and want the translation immediately, simply **tap the microphone button a second time** to stop right away!

---

## 4. What to Do If Speech Is Misunderstood

If the app mishears a word or writes something incorrect:
1. **Do not panic.** Background noise or overlapping voices can sometimes cause a mistake.
2. Tap the **Microphone Button** again.
3. Repeat your sentence more slowly and clearly.
4. If a word continues to be misheard, rephrase using simpler words (e.g. say *"दवा"* instead of *"औषधि"*).

---

## 5. What to Do If the Microphone Fails

If tapping the microphone does nothing or shows an error:
1. **Check System Permissions:**
   - Swipe down from top $\to$ check if Android microphone privacy dot is green.
   - Go to Android **Settings** $\to$ **Apps** $\to$ **Chrome** $\to$ **Permissions** $\to$ **Microphone** $\to$ select **"Allow while using app"**.
2. **Audio Conflict:**
   - If another app (like WhatsApp, YouTube, or Voice Recorder) is open in the background, close all open apps from the Android app switcher.
3. **Quick Refresh:**
   - Pull down the screen to refresh the app, or close and relaunch Bhasha Setu from your home screen.

---

## 6. What to Do If Translation Is Uncertain

1. Look at the translation card on the screen.
2. If the translation badge is **Yellow** or says **"Review Recommended"**:
   - The system is warning you that the translation may be approximate.
   - Read the translated text aloud or check the Ol Chiki / Hindi text with the speaker.
3. If the meaning is still ambiguous, tap the **Quick Phrases** button to use pre-verified sentences that are guaranteed 100% accurate.

---

## 7. When Human Verification Is Strictly Required

> [!CAUTION]
> **MANDATORY CLINICAL & LEGAL SAFETY RULE:**  
> Never administer medication, prescribe dosage, or make life-safety decisions solely based on machine translation!

Human verification is **MANDATORY** in the following situations:
- **Prescriptions and Dosing:** Any instruction involving drug names, milligrams, milliliters, or dosage frequency (e.g., *"Take 1 tablet twice daily after meals"*).
- **Allergies & Contraindications:** Confirming patient allergic reactions or medical history.
- **Consent for Medical Procedures:** Explanation of clinical procedures, immunization forms, or surgical referrals.
- **Emergency Symptoms:** Severe chest pain, respiratory distress, convulsions, or high fever with unconsciousness.

In all these scenarios:
1. Show the screen to a bilingual colleague, tribal volunteer, or certified nurse.
2. Verify understanding with the patient or student using non-verbal confirmation and clear physical gestures.
